How the days work

  • Ten fixed groups of 6–7, mixed disciplines; roles rotate each block (coder, checker, question writers, interviewer, reporter).
  • No scheduled breaks: pause during the exercise blocks when it suits your group, but be back at the fixed peer-review times.
  • Peer review: during the exercises your group writes three questions with worked solutions; then you interview your partner group (1 ↔ 2, 3 ↔ 4, …) and they interview you.
  • Plenary: two groups are drawn at random; one interviews the other in front of the class, then the lecturer takes up the hardest questions.
  • Daily diary: on Mentimeter, three things you learned and three you are not yet familiar with.

Grading

50 %

In-class activity

Each group is scored 0–4 per day on how actively it works. Every member present gets the group’s score. Effort and thinking count, not being right.

50 %

Final assignment

Individual, after the course, scored out of 100.

To pass

70 % and 5 of 6 days

A weighted total of at least 70 %, and attendance on at least five of the six days.

Final assignment

Individual · after the course

Exercise collection

The same assignment for everybody: seven independent problems with everyday settings (free throws, help-desk calls, ice cream, restaurant reviews, driving tests, a kitchen scale, a bakery), each with its own small dataset. Most of the code is provided; you fill in the missing pieces. Getting stuck in one problem does not block the others.

If you must miss a day

You may miss at most one day. Tell the lecturer before the day starts, then submit that day’s in-class exercises and Parts A and B of its extra exercises within a week. The make-up is graded 0–4 and counts as your score for that day. Each day’s make-up page (linked above) has the details.
