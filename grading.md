# Grading and attendance

**Bayesian statistics for PhD researchers · Aalborg University · 2026**

## Summary

| Component | Weight | How it is assessed |
|---|---|---|
| **In-class activity** | 50 % | The lecturer grades each **group's** activeness for each course day. Every member of the group gets the group's score for that day. |
| **Final assignment** | 50 % | An individual exercise collection after the course, scored out of 100. |
| **Course result** | | **Pass** if the weighted total is **at least 70 %** and the attendance rule below is met. Otherwise **fail**. |

Final course score = 0.5 × in-class score (%) + 0.5 × final-assignment score (%).

## 1. In-class activity (50 %)

We expect every student to take an active part in the course:

- work seriously on the exercises and think about them, rather than waiting for the solution;
- take part in the group's question set;
- take part in the peer interviews, both asking and answering;
- answer questions in the plenary when your group is drawn;
- ask questions of your group, of other groups and of the lecturer.

**Getting things right is not what is graded. Effort, thinking and participation are.** A wrong answer that shows real thinking, or a good question about something you do not understand, counts in your favour.

### How it is graded

- At the end of each course day, the lecturer gives **each group a score from 0 to 4** for that day. All present members of the group receive that score.
- The score is based on what the lecturer and any teaching assistants observe during the exercise sessions and the peer-review sessions, and on the group's question set.
- Being drawn or not drawn in the plenary does not by itself raise or lower a score.
- The grading is a **professional judgement and is partly subjective**. The descriptors below make it as consistent and transparent as possible.

| Score | Descriptor (for the group, for that day) |
|---|---|
| **4 — Excellent** | The group works seriously on the exercises for the whole session and discusses them. Members ask questions when stuck. The question set is thoughtful, its solutions are worked out, and it targets something the group found difficult. In peer review the group asks and answers with real arguments, and every member contributes. |
| **3 — Good** | Clear effort and discussion on the exercises. A reasonable question set with solutions. Constructive participation in peer review. There are minor gaps, for example one member mostly passive or a routine question set. |
| **2 — Adequate** | The exercises are attempted, but with limited discussion or thinking. The question set is superficial, or its solutions are incomplete. Participation in peer review is perfunctory. |
| **1 — Weak** | Little visible effort or discussion. The question set is missing or copied. The group is late for, or largely passive in, the peer review. |
| **0 — No participation** | No meaningful participation, or no group member present. |

### Your in-class score

Your in-class score is the average of your six daily scores, converted to a percentage (score ÷ 4 × 100). For example, daily scores of 3, 4, 3, 3, 4, 3 average 3.33, which is **83 %**.

- **A day you attended:** you get your group's score for that day.
- **A day you missed** (with advance notice, section 2): the lecturer grades your **make-up submission** individually on the same 0–4 scale, judging effort and your own reasoning, and that score counts for the day.

Attendance itself is not graded. A missed day without advance notice or without a make-up submission does not score 0; instead the attendance requirement is not met (section 2).

### Transparency

- The lecturer keeps a short note with each daily group score.
- Groups can see their running scores, for example at the end of Days 2 and 4, so they can respond while there is still time.
- If you think a score is wrong, raise it with the lecturer within a week.

## 2. Attendance

- You must attend the exercise sessions on **at least 5 of the 6 course days**.
- If you have to miss a day, you must **inform the lecturer in advance** (before the day starts; in case of sudden illness, as early as possible). You must then submit, individually and normally within one week:
  1. your own solutions to that day's **in-class exercises** (the day's class notebook, core parts);
  2. that day's **extra exercises** (`day-N/extra/`, Parts A and B).

  The lecturer grades the make-up submission on the 0–4 scale (section 1), and that score counts as your in-class score for the missed day.

  See `day-N/day-N-makeup.md` for details.
- If you miss a day without informing the lecturer in advance, or without a make-up submission, the attendance requirement is not met.
- Missing **more than one day** means the attendance requirement is not met and the course cannot be passed. The only exception is documented exceptional circumstances under the doctoral school's rules.
- Attendance means taking part in the exercise and peer-review sessions of the day, and submitting the daily diary.

## 3. Final assignment (50 %)

The final assignment is individual and is done after the course. Everybody does the same **exercise collection**: seven independent problems with everyday settings, each with its own small dataset. In Problems 1–4 most of the code is provided and you fill in the missing pieces of the R code and of the Stan programs; in Problems 5–7 you write the code and the Stan programs yourself, with hints. In every problem you report the requested results. See [final-assessment/README.md](final-assessment/README.md).

It is scored out of **100 points**: **20 points** in each of the five areas of [final-assessment/rubric.md](final-assessment/rubric.md):

- model formulation;
- Bayesian reasoning;
- computation;
- model checking and sensitivity;
- communication.

You may be asked to explain your submission in a short conversation. If you cannot explain a central part of your work, the points for that part are not awarded.

## 4. The pass rule in practice

| In-class score | Final-assignment score needed for 70 % overall |
|---|---|
| 100 % | 40 |
| 90 % | 50 |
| 80 % | 60 |
| 70 % | 70 |
| 60 % | 80 |
| 50 % | 90 |

Active participation every day therefore matters as much as the final assignment.

## 5. Your own work

Discussing ideas is encouraged. Submitted solutions, code and reports must be your own. You may use AI assistants and do not need to declare it, but you are responsible for everything you submit and must be able to explain it.

## 6. Deadline

The final assignment must be uploaded to the Moodle assignment on the course page, as one zip file named `firstname_lastname.zip`, by **13 December 2026 at 23:55** (Danish time). **The deadline is strict:** late submissions are not accepted and the Moodle upload closes at that time. Upload early; a technical problem at 23:50 is not a reason for an extension.

Make-up submissions for a missed day are due as agreed with the lecturer when you report the absence.
